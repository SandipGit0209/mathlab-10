Key language

Think like a money changer

Buying rate

The rate used when the bank or money changer buys foreign currency from you.

Selling rate

The rate used when the bank or money changer sells foreign currency to you.

NPR → foreign currency

You are buying foreign currency, so use the selling rate.

Foreign = NPR ÷ selling rate

Foreign → NPR

You are selling foreign currency, so use the buying rate.

NPR = foreign × buying rate

Cross currency

When a direct rate is not given, use NPR as the common bridge.

Source → NPR → Target

Chain rule idea

Sell the source currency to get NPR, then use those NPR to buy the target currency.

Target = Source × source buying rate ÷ target selling rate

Which rates?

The bank buys the currency you give and sells the currency you receive.

Source: buying • Target: selling

Spread

Selling rate is normally greater than buying rate. The difference is the spread.

Devaluation of NPR

Nepali currency becomes weaker relative to foreign currency. In SEE-style problems, the NPR-per-foreign-currency exchange rate increases by the stated percentage.

New rate = Old rate × (1 + r/100)

Revaluation of NPR

Nepali currency becomes stronger relative to foreign currency. The NPR-per-foreign-currency exchange rate decreases by the stated percentage.

New rate = Old rate × (1 − r/100)

Exam shortcut

If $1 = Rs. 120 and NPR is devalued by 5%, $1 becomes Rs. 126. If NPR is revalued by 5%, $1 becomes Rs. 114.

Detailed working
Why not use buying rate twice?
Because the bank's role changes. It buys what you hand over, but it sells what you ask to receive.
Chain rule pattern

Write units so they cancel

Source × (NPR / Source) × (Target / NPR) = Target
The source unit cancels first, then NPR cancels, leaving only the target currency. This is why the chain rule is reliable.

Learn by comparison

Same old rate, opposite effects

5% devaluation

If $1 = Rs. 120:

120 × 1.05 = Rs. 126

More NPR are required for the same $1.

5% revaluation

If $1 = Rs. 120:

120 × 0.95 = Rs. 114

Fewer NPR are required for the same $1.
